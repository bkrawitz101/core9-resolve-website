# Executive production brief

## The business goal
Publish amusing, five-minute fictional audio conversations that attract a broad audience, prompt weekly visits to CORE9's own website, encourage exploration of the complete archive, and introduce interested listeners to a genuine expert consultation. These are **not lectures, testimonials or advertisements**.

## Show identities

### Business Unusual — Where Business Problems Get Personal
**Primary audience:** business owners, founders, entrepreneurs; also leaders and partners. **Hosts:** Max Logic (confident, analytical, often too certain) and Marsha Chaos (perceptive, unpredictable, challenges assumptions). **Setting:** familiar business crises and arguments. Guest experts are fictional, funny and occasionally wrong. The owner is not always right or wrong.

### Mr. Right & Mrs. Maybe — He Has All the Answers. She Has All the Questions.
**Primary audience:** everyone living and working with other people, including employees, employed managers, couples, families, friends and partners. The recurring duo disagree and reverse roles; neither is always wise. Domestic comedy is the center, but workplace issues can surface from the **employee's or employed manager's** perspective. Avoid stereotypes about gender, marriage, work or age.

## Inter-show bridge
Same human problem, different lived experience. Example: business owner complains employees won't decide; at home an employed manager complains boss won't allow decisions. Use paired themes selectively; each episode must stand alone. No obligation to listen to both.

## Fixed operating decisions
- 2 total episodes/week: 1 per show, ~5 minutes each
- Audio-only initially; no animated characters or video production
- 40 initial topics = 20 weeks at that cadence
- The weekly **email links to the CORE9 episode page**, not directly to an MP3 or third-party directory
- Episode page offers player, transcript/summary, next/related episodes, both show archives and consultation CTA
- Publish externally via podcast hosting RSS to Spotify, Apple Podcasts, Amazon Music and other supported directories, with a link back to CORE9
- Maintain minimum 4 weeks of completed audio and 10 planned future topics; batch produce
- Founder reviews initial pilot samples and major changes; producer handles routine QA

## Responsibility matrix
**Founder/brand owner:** supplies booking URL, domain, approved identity/art, accounts and any final brand corrections; approves first pilot for each show and launch.
**Production manager:** owns scripts, voice generation, edit, metadata, editorial calendar, QA, scheduled releases, monthly topic replenishment.
**Developer:** builds CMS templates, episode ingestion, player, archives, SEO, analytics, email integration, podcast links, automation and permissions; documents and tests.
**Podcast host:** stores audio and creates separate RSS feeds for each show; directories consume feeds after initial submission.

## First 30 days (relative schedule)
**Week 1 — Design and setup:** confirm owner-supplied inputs, lock page hierarchy and creative bible, select podcast host, create show accounts, design cover art and two sample episode pages, establish booking link.
**Week 2 — Build and pilot:** implement responsive pages, CMS schema, players, newsletter templates, tracking and booking. Produce two pilots (one per show); revise voice, pacing and prompt. Build 4-week buffer.
**Week 3 — Validate:** QA on desktop/mobile; RSS/feed checks; submit shows to directories; test subscription, unsubscribe, email links, analytics, fallback and accessibility; schedule initial releases.
**Week 4 — Launch:** publish first two episodes, send one email per episode or a combined digest if list policy prefers, monitor analytics and errors, replenish pipeline.

**Dependency:** directory review may take longer than launch; launch on owned website first, add directory badges when verified live.

## What is not included / cannot be guessed
Exact CORE9 domain/path, consultation booking URL, contact address, podcast host account, email service, privacy policy URLs, consent language specific to your jurisdiction, licensed voice assets, existing analytics account, logo/approved imagery, and any unverified missing source chapters. Use placeholders in draft only, never in production.
