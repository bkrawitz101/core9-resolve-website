# What If Every Completion Brought You Closer to an Extraordinary Achievement?

**Function 9 — Completion, Renewal & Creation**

You have the potential to recognize when something has fulfilled its purpose, discover what remains valuable, and open a new chapter without carrying everything from the past into the future.

Function 9 is the energy of renewal and creation.

It recognizes possibilities where others see endings. It can bring people together, inspire action, and help new ideas emerge. Its generous impulse to help is powerful—but it can become exhausting when every problem feels like a personal responsibility.

The deeper gift of Function 9 is not rescuing everyone or keeping everything alive.

It is knowing what deserves to continue, what is ready to end, and what can now be created.

This is where the nine functions come together. You are no longer simply reacting to life. You can recognize a completed cycle, learn from it, and consciously participate in creating the next one.

And sometimes the most creative decision is not to do more. It is to let something end.

## Try it in real life — The door you were afraid to close

Imagine you've spent fifteen years building something meaningful.

Perhaps it's an organization, a community project, or a business that once brought people together and gave you a strong sense of purpose.

You remember the excitement of the early days. The friendships. The achievements. The people whose lives changed because of it.

But things are different now.

Participation is declining. The original purpose no longer inspires the same enthusiasm. Maintaining the project requires more money, more persuasion, and more of your personal energy.

People still come to you with requests.

“We can't stop now. So many people depend on this.”

You feel responsible.

One evening, you look through photographs from the project's earliest years.

You remember what made it extraordinary.

Then a surprising thought appears:

“What if the most important thing we created wasn't this organization—but everything it made possible?”

Perhaps the original form has completed its purpose.

Perhaps something new is waiting.

### Choice 1: Keep everything going because so many people depend on it.
**Outcome — The past becomes an obligation**
You continue funding, organizing, and encouraging participation. People appreciate your loyalty, but the project gradually consumes resources that could support something more relevant. Your commitment to its history makes it difficult to recognize what has changed.
*Reflection: Are you preserving something because it is still alive—or because you cannot bear to let it end?*

### Choice 2: Walk away completely and leave everything behind.
**Outcome — An ending without integration**
You close the project and experience immediate relief. Yet valuable relationships, lessons, and possibilities are lost because you never explored what could be carried into a new chapter.
*Reflection: What deserves to be released, and what deserves to be carried forward?*

### Choice 3: Honor what was accomplished, complete the cycle, and explore what wants to emerge.
**Outcome — An ending becomes a creative beginning**
You bring people together to celebrate what the project achieved. You listen to what they have learned and what they need now. Some activities end, others find new homes, and a few people begin developing an entirely new idea. You are no longer responsible for keeping the old structure alive. You have helped create the conditions for something new to grow.
*Reflection: What could become possible if you allowed a completed chapter to end with gratitude rather than guilt?*

## Personal — The life waiting behind the old identity

Imagine you've spent most of your adult life being known for one thing.

You're the successful professional. The dependable organizer. The person who always knows what to do.

People admire your accomplishments.

But recently, something has changed.

The work that once excited you no longer holds the same meaning. New interests are appearing: writing, art, travel, perhaps an entirely different way of living.

You feel curious, but also uncomfortable.

“If I'm no longer the person I've always been, who am I?”

You open an old box filled with awards, certificates, and photographs.

You're proud of what you've accomplished.

But you suddenly realize that your history doesn't have to become your future.

### Choice 1: Continue living through the identity that once brought success.
**Outcome — A successful past becomes a limitation**
You preserve your familiar routines and reputation. Others continue recognizing you, but your emerging interests remain unexplored. Life feels increasingly predictable.
*Reflection: Which part of your identity are you maintaining because others expect it?*

### Choice 2: Reject your old life and reinvent yourself completely.
**Outcome — Freedom without continuity**
You abandon familiar activities and distance yourself from your previous achievements. The change feels exciting at first, but you may lose valuable knowledge, relationships, and experience that could enrich your next chapter.
*Reflection: Must becoming someone new require rejecting who you have been?*

### Choice 3: Honor your achievements and give your emerging interests room to grow.
**Outcome — You become the author of your next chapter**
You recognize that your experience remains valuable even when your role changes. You explore new interests, develop fresh relationships, and allow your life to evolve without demanding that every new activity become another achievement.
*Reflection: What would you love to create if you no longer had to prove who you are?*

## Relationships — The family tradition nobody wanted to lose

Imagine your family has celebrated the same holiday in the same house for decades.

Every year, everyone gathers around the table. There are familiar dishes, stories, photographs, and traditions passed down through generations.

But the family has changed.

Children have grown up. Some relatives have moved away. Others have new responsibilities and families of their own.

Organizing the celebration has become increasingly difficult.

One year, several people say they cannot attend.

Someone responds:

“If we stop doing this, we'll lose what makes us a family.”

You feel the sadness behind those words.

But you also wonder:

“Is our connection held together by this particular tradition—or by the love that created it?”

### Choice 1: Insist that everyone continue the tradition exactly as before.
**Outcome — Togetherness becomes pressure**
Some family members attend out of obligation. Others withdraw. What once represented love begins creating resentment because the tradition no longer accommodates the family’s changing lives.
*Reflection: Are you protecting the meaning of the tradition—or only its familiar form?*

### Choice 2: Stop celebrating together because organizing it has become too difficult.
**Outcome — The burden ends, but so does an opportunity**
Everyone gains freedom from the old arrangements. Yet the family loses a valuable occasion for connection without exploring whether a different celebration could work.
*Reflection: What is worth preserving even when its original form no longer fits?*

### Choice 3: Discover what everyone values and create a tradition suited to the family today.
**Outcome — The connection finds a new form**
You invite everyone to share what makes the celebration meaningful. Some traditions remain, others change, and new ones emerge. The family discovers that its connection can evolve without losing its history.
*Reflection: How might love express itself differently as the people we care about grow and change?*

## Business — The product that built the company

Imagine your company became successful because of one extraordinary product.

For years, customers loved it. Employees were proud of it. The product's success financed your growth and established your reputation.

But the market has changed.

Customers are looking for something different. Sales are declining, and competitors are introducing new solutions.

Your team has several promising ideas, but most of the company's resources remain committed to the original product.

At a leadership meeting, someone says:

“We can't abandon the product that made us who we are.”

Another replies:

“But if we keep investing everything in yesterday's success, what will we have left for tomorrow?”

The room falls silent.

You realize that the company may not need to abandon its purpose.

It may need to release the form in which that purpose was once expressed.

### Choice 1: Invest more resources in protecting the original product.
**Outcome — Yesterday’s success consumes tomorrow’s possibilities**
You increase promotion and defend the familiar offering. These actions may temporarily support sales, but the company’s resources remain tied to a declining opportunity while promising alternatives receive little attention.
*Reflection: Are you investing in future value—or defending a previous achievement?*

### Choice 2: Discontinue the product immediately and pursue every new opportunity.
**Outcome — Change without direction**
You free resources quickly, but employees and customers struggle with the abrupt transition. The company spreads its attention across too many ideas without learning what made its original success valuable.
*Reflection: What lessons from the completed cycle could help guide the next one?*

### Choice 3: Recognize what has completed its purpose and build the next chapter around what remains valuable.
**Outcome — The company renews its capacity to create**
You examine customer needs, the company’s strengths, and the results of past decisions. You develop a responsible transition, protect what remains viable, release outdated commitments, and give selected new ideas the resources to develop. The company’s history becomes a source of learning rather than a restriction on its future.
*Reflection: What could your organization create if it stopped treating every ending as a failure?*

## The deeper layers: all nine functions working together

Function 9 completes a cycle, but it does not operate independently. Every new cycle can draw on the capacities developed throughout CORE9 RESOLVE™. These are not nine additional tasks the visitor must perform. They are interconnected perspectives that can reveal different aspects of the same situation. In the business story, the decision to retire a product involves understanding the market (3), choosing priorities (4), protecting relationships (2), identifying viable opportunities (5), sustaining the people involved (6), finding a realistic path forward (7), and organizing resources (8), all in service of a future worth creating (1). The visitor sees three connected energies within the current world, while the deeper interpretation can draw on all nine functions.

1. **Strategy:** What future is now worth creating?
2. **Diplomacy & Relationships:** Whose perspectives and relationships matter?
3. **Analysis & Systems:** What is actually happening, and how do the parts connect?
4. **Knowledge, Goals & Priorities:** What have we learned, and what deserves attention?
5. **Initiation & Expansion:** What contribution or possibility is ready to grow?
6. **Psychology, Beauty & Fulfillment:** Will this new chapter nourish the people living it?
7. **Realization & Living Energy:** What activates the energy and possibilities needed to move forward?
8. **Materialization & Prosperity:** What resources and structures will make it sustainable?
9. **Completion, Renewal & Creation:** What can be completed, released, carried forward, or newly created?

## Recognize it in yourself

What are you still carrying because it once mattered—even though its purpose may now be complete?

Function 9 invites you to recognize that endings are not necessarily failures.

A completed chapter can leave behind wisdom, gratitude, stronger relationships, valuable resources, and the freedom to create something different.

You don't need to rescue every person, preserve every structure, or pursue every new idea.

You can honor what was, recognize what is alive now, and choose what deserves to emerge next.

**Creation becomes possible when you make room for it.**
